# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: academic peers and evaluators, promotion and hiring committees, department chairs, deans, and faculty at peer institutions. They arrive to assess Adam's credibility and record, often quickly and often comparing him against other candidates. The site must hold up to a skeptical, time-pressed expert reader.

Secondary: industry and funding partners (EcoCAR, GCxN, capstone sponsors), prospective students and advisees, and speaking/press contacts.

## Product Purpose

A personal site for Adam W. Duran (PE, PMP), engineering educator and program architect at Colorado School of Mines (adamwduran.com). It exists to establish credibility and provide proof of a substantial, verifiable track record: programs built from scratch, $10M+ in funded research, 252 students served, 30+ publications, and a decade of NREL/DOE vehicle-technology R&D.

Success is an academic or institutional evaluator leaving the site taking Adam seriously, with the record doing the persuading. The site backs the promotion case and the broader professional reputation rather than chasing inbound leads.

## Brand Personality

Builder, pragmatic, industry-connected. Voice is confident, evidence-led, and concrete, with no hype or overclaiming: maker energy over ivory-tower formality. The brief is "decent, needs boldness," and the reference point is studio- and agency-grade personal sites: strong typography, real motion, design-literate craft. Aim for that level of polish and ambition. Credibility is earned through proof and precision, not through playing it safe.

## Anti-references

- The generic university-CMS faculty homepage: sidebar nav, stock photos, a cramped CV dump, dated typography.
- SaaS/startup landing tropes: gradient hero, the hero-metric template, identical icon-heading-text card grids, tracked-uppercase eyebrow kickers.

## Design Principles

1. Proof over claims. Lead with the record (named programs, real numbers, outcomes) instead of asserting excellence.
2. Studio-grade craft. Hold the bar of a design-studio personal site: deliberate typography, real motion, and finish. Ambition is welcome; the proof is what keeps it credible.
3. Editorial rigor. The site should read like a well-edited publication; typographic hierarchy and restraint carry the authority.
4. Respect the skim. Evaluators scan and compare; every screen leads with its strongest evidence and has one clear primary point.
5. Preserve the identity. The navy/steel-plus-gold palette and serif/sans system are the established brand. Evolve and sharpen; do not reinvent.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AAA where feasible: push body text to 7:1 contrast where the palette allows, falling back to solid AA (4.5:1) where AAA would force off-brand colors. Full keyboard navigation with visible focus states, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honored (already implemented via the `card-lift` pattern). Keep the palette color-blind-safe (navy/gold is reasonably robust; verify gold-on-light combinations for contrast). Public, .edu-adjacent audience, so accessibility is part of the credibility, not an afterthought.
